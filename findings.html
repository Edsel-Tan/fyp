Six ideas, one tape

Every prediction-market idea in the brainstorm rests on the same substrate: a Polymarket/Kalshi trade tape that begins 21 May 2026 and an order book that is mostly not there. That single fact reorders the recommendation.

Source lum.id/findata REST Equity DB 2,012,954 bars / 4,628 symbols PM probes 583 markets, 32 resolved tapes

Headline

The idea the brainstorm recommends is the one the data least supports.

fyp_ideas.md lands on idea 4 — RL market-making on prediction-market order books, partly because it "plugs directly into the lab's existing Polymarket data". It does not. Polymarket order-book capture stopped on 29 July 2026, the snapshots that exist are spaced ~3 minutes apart, and a large share of them carry depth on only one side or no depth at all. Market-making is a microstructure problem being asked to run on data with neither the resolution nor the continuity to support it.

Meanwhile idea 1 (informed-trader decomposition) and idea 3 (cross-venue mispricing) are both far better provisioned than the document assumes — trades carry taker wallet addresses, and the vendor already publishes cross-venue market matches, which is normally the hardest part of idea 3.

Constraint 01

The prediction-market tape starts 21 May 2026 and is still running.

Across a 583-market sample, no captured Polymarket trade predates 2026-05-21T19:49Z, and the newest reaches 2026-08-16T10:56Z — about 12 hours before this audit. The boundary is the vendor's capture start, not market age: markets that opened in December 2025 still have no tape before 21 May.

This is the governing constraint on ideas 1, 2 and 3. You have ~3 months of history that grows a day per day, which is workable for a thesis running to spring but rules out any design needing the 2024 election period. Kalshi's tape reaches back further — trades appear from April 2026 on the market I traced — so a Kalshi-anchored study buys extra history.

Watch the volume field. The Fed June market advertises $43,061,416 of volume; the captured tape over its whole life holds $1,295,360 across 2,635 trades. The field is Polymarket's lifetime figure and is not comparable to anything you can compute locally. Several markets showing tens of millions in volume returned zero captured trades. Rank markets by captured trade count, never by volume.

The order book is the weak link, and it fails differently on each venue.

Kalshi's book is genuinely good data — perfectly regular 5-minute snapshots, always two-sided, ~38 price levels a side. But it only starts around 30 July, so there is roughly a fortnight of it. Polymarket has a longer window and richer depth, but capture has stopped and the snapshots are unreliable.

Either way, 5-minute snapshots cannot support a market-making agent. Quoting, inventory management and adverse selection all live at second-or-finer resolution; at 300 seconds you are not modelling a book, you are interpolating one. That is what blocks idea 4 in its stated form.

The live stream does not work. /prediction-markets/stream never returns HTTP headers at all — it simply hangs, on both condition_ids and asset_ids subscriptions. The control, /quotes/stream, answers 200 immediately and delivers crypto ticks at ~78 ms. So the vendor's prediction-market streaming path cannot currently be used to capture your own high-frequency book.

Finding 01

Concentration in a single market reproduces the paper's premise almost exactly.

I pulled the complete captured tape for the resolved market "Will there be no change in Fed interest rates after the June 2026 meeting?" — 2,635 trades, 811 unique takers — and measured how notional is distributed across wallets.

93.2%of notional from the top 3% of takers
84.4%from the top 1% — just 8 wallets
0.972Gini across taker notional
1median trades per taker

Gómez-Cram et al. report that ~3% of Polymarket traders drive price discovery. On this market, 3% of takers supply 93% of the flow. The premise of idea 1 is directly observable in this dataset, which is a meaningful de-risking of that project.

And the crowd shows a textbook favourite-longshot bias

The market resolved YES and traded near 0.98 for most of its captured life. Splitting takers by trade size shows who was on which side:

Small takers bought the 2-cent longshot roughly two times in three; the largest takers sided with the favourite. Per-trade ROI follows: the bottom nine size deciles all lose, the top decile is the only one that does not.

Finding 02

Scaled to 32 resolved markets, that edge does not survive.

I repeated the exercise on every cleanly-resolved market I could find inside the tape window with at least 150 captured trades — 32 markets spanning Fed decisions, NBA playoffs, Bitcoin thresholds and three national elections. Pooled by notional, the result reverses:

+1.33%pooled ROI, top 3% of wallets
+2.51%pooled ROI, bottom 90% — the crowd wins
20/32markets where whales beat the crowd (62%)
t = 0.16on the mean per-market difference

The pooled figure is also fragile to a single observation. Removing one market — a Seoul mayoral contest where a handful of small wallets caught a +148% move — flips the crowd from +2.51% to −4.08% while barely moving the whales (+0.91%). Per-market ROI differences have a standard deviation of 32.7% against a mean of +0.90%.

This is the useful result, not a failed one. It says the informed-trader effect is real in direction (62% sign test, and whales beat the crowd once one outlier is removed) but hopelessly underpowered at n=32 with three months of tape. It also says trader ROI is the wrong estimator — the literature measures contribution to price discovery, not profit, and profit in a market that opens at 0.98 is nearly all noise.

For idea 1 that converts a vague research question into a concrete methodological plan: scale to hundreds of markets as the tape accumulates, and use a price-discovery measure rather than PnL.

Do not label "informed" wallets from the leaderboard. Its PnL is computed over each wallet's entire history, so using it to classify traders in a market you are forecasting leaks the outcome — straightforward look-ahead bias. It is also stale: of the top 100 wallets I sampled, only 48% have traded since the tape begins, and 13 last traded in November 2024. Derive informedness from the tape, on a rolling backward-looking window.

Finding 03

Cross-venue matching is already done for you — across four venues, not two.

/prediction-markets/matched-pairs returns, for a Polymarket market, its counterparts elsewhere with a similarity score and an exact/related label. Of 583 sampled Polymarket markets, 148 (25%) have at least one match and 140 (24%) have an exact one. Counterparties break down as Kalshi 184, Limitless 17, Opinion 3.

That is a real gift and a real problem. The gift: idea 3's data-engineering burden mostly evaporates, and a four-venue study is more interesting than the two-venue one the brainstorm describes. The problem: semantic matching was going to be a good chunk of the contribution. If the vendor hands you the pairs, idea 3 needs its novelty somewhere else — the obvious candidates being the structural asymmetry the document already identifies (CFTC-regulated venue versus not), or auditing the matches themselves, since a 97-similarity "exact" pair can still have materially different resolution criteria.

The equity database

Sound, with two defects that will silently corrupt any returns work.

data/findata.sqlite (248 MB) holds 2,012,954 daily bars for 4,628 symbols from 19 Aug 2024 to 14 Aug 2026, plus 6,270 latest-quarter and 72,525 historical fundamental records. Median 494 bars per symbol is exactly right for two years of US sessions. Internal OHLC consistency is good: 157 violations in two million rows.

The two defects both bite returns calculations:

  1. No split adjustment. adj_close and vwap are 100% NULL, as the earlier session flagged. Screening for extreme one-day moves above a $1 price floor leaves 282 symbols (6.1%) with discontinuities, and the ratios cluster hard at exactly 2.0× (81 events) — real splits, not volatility. Fetch /splits/{symbol} and back-adjust before computing anything.
  2. Sub-penny prices detonate return series. 6,974 rows close below $0.001 and 109 close at exactly zero. Computed naively, daily returns reach +3,749,999% and the cross-sectional mean return is +400%. With a $1 floor on both legs the distribution becomes sane — median 0.00%, 1st percentile −8.9%, 99th +10.4%. Apply a price floor and a liquidity screen before any modelling.

Composition is worth knowing before designing a cross-sectional study: 1,449 of the covered names are Financial Services (many closed-end funds and trusts), median market cap is $1.16 B, and only 55 names exceed $200 B. Fundamentals fields are a clean 25-item set — revenue, EBITDA, FCF, capex, debt, equity — quarterly, reaching back to 1994 for long-lived filers.

Recommendation

Lead with idea 1, and start capturing your own book today either way.

Idea 1 is now the strongest candidate, inverting the brainstorm's ranking. Every input it needs exists and is live: trade-level tape with taker wallets, wallet profiles carrying behavioural style flags (is_whale, is_market_maker, is_contrarian…), position and activity history, and top-holder snapshots. I have already reproduced its central premise, found a clean secondary result in the longshot bias, and established that the naive version of its test fails — which is exactly the kind of groundwork that makes a first supervision meeting productive.

Idea 3 is the strong second and pairs naturally with idea 1: both are measurement studies on the same tape, so the data engineering is shared. Its open question is novelty, not feasibility.

Idea 4 should not be committed to on current data. If the RL-market-making angle matters to you — and it is the one that best satisfies the "RL for trading" half of the brief — the salvage is to run your own order-book capture now, polling Polymarket's and Kalshi's public CLOB endpoints directly rather than through this vendor, whose Polymarket capture has stopped and whose streaming route is broken. Every week you delay is a week of book you cannot recover later. Six months of self-captured second-resolution book would make idea 4 genuinely strong by spring; without it the project has no simulator to learn against.

What I would do next

  1. Start a book capture daemon this week — Kalshi and Polymarket public APIs, 1–5 s polling, append-only. Cheap to run, impossible to backfill.
  2. Mirror the trade tape locally, paging by time window, for every market with captured trades. It is small, and it stops you re-hitting a vendor that has already had a multi-hour outage during this project.
  3. Rebuild the idea-1 test around price discovery — information shares or a Hasbrouck-style decomposition — rather than trader ROI, and let market count grow with the tape.
  4. Audit twenty matched pairs by hand before betting idea 3 on them; confirm that "exact" survives a reading of both resolution rules.
  5. Back-adjust the equity bars for splits and add a price/liquidity screen, so ideas 5 and 6 start from a clean panel.

Scripts for the reproducible parts are in scripts/ — pm_informed_vs_crowd.py, pm_matched_pairs.py, pm_tape_coverage.py. All require LUMID_TOKEN in the environment.

Caveats on my own numbers: the 32-market panel is a convenience sample drawn from keyword searches, not the full market universe, so it is not a clean population estimate; single-market results come from one Fed market that was near-resolved throughout, which compresses every ROI toward zero; and API list routes cap at 500–1000 rows, so a handful of the busiest windows may be truncated despite the time-paging.
